In a Sea of Stars

An uncertain archive of things that might be called a star — a starfish, a trophy, a song, a stranger's face, a chair.

You drift through it in a paper boat, folded from grid paper the way a child folds a first ship: small, light, not quite sure where it is going.

Each paper star carries one image. Come close, and it shows its own light.

How the stars glow

  • Brightness — how strongly three machines read the image as a star.
  • Colour — the image's own colour, softened into light.
  • A slow drift between lavender and mist blue — the machines disagree.
  • A quiet pearl glow — no one could read it yet. Unknown is not the same as unrelated.

Three ways of seeing

  • Teachable Machine learned my own five categories — Direct, Named, Represented, Associated, Unrelated — from this archive. It has learned my habits as much as it has learned stars.
  • CLIP compares each picture with written sentences about what a star can mean.
  • MobileNet names the objects it recognises, then looks for them in a small star vocabulary. When it finds nothing, the star simply stays unnamed.

Their readings are guesses, not verdicts. Retrieve any star — bright or quiet — to see its photograph, the three readings, and what other visitors saw in it.

Not everything that matters has to shine.
